# Climate series part 3: Your Summer in 2050 — design

Folder essay at `essays/summer/`, order 18. Reader picks a UK city up front;
the entire essay re-parameterizes (charts, numbers, sentences). City persists
in the URL hash, like part 2's footprint state.

## Structure

1. Masthead: city picker as the hero interaction, ~12 UK city buttons
   (London default), no scrolling needed to commit.
2. Analog reveal (scrolly): map panel drawing an arc from your city to its
   2050 analog (Bastin et al. 2019: "London 2050 feels like Barcelona today").
   Steps: city now → arc travels → analog lands → caveat (analogs are
   averages; extremes shift more than means).
3. Hot days & tropical nights (scrolly, centerpiece): dot calendar of
   June–August, one dot per day; dots ignite moving from the 1990s baseline
   to the 2050s (days over 25/28°C), tropical nights (>20°C) fill in second.
4. Your hottest day (scrolly): the city's actual record (e.g. 40.3°C, July
   2022) placed against the 2050s summer distribution; the record becomes an
   ordinary hot day.
5. Seasons shifting (scrolly): circular year-clock; spring markers rotate
   earlier, frost window shrinks, growing season stretches. May stay
   UK-generic, disclosed.
6. Rain flip (scrolly): seasonal rainfall bars morph; summers drier, winters
   wetter, intensity up. UKCP18 regional, mapped city→region.
7. Close: street-level meaning (rails, schools, gardens); links to parts 1–2;
   tease part 4 (which 2050 you get is decided by the levers in part 4).

## Data (research-first)

- Analog pairs: Bastin et al. 2019 figshare dataset (520 cities, RCP4.5-ish).
  Fetch, confirm UK coverage, restrict the picker to covered cities.
- Hot days / tropical nights: UKCP18 via Met Office city packs and
  local-authority summaries; hand-gathered per city with per-field citations.
  Slowest task, verified hardest.
- Hottest-day records: Met Office station records, hand-curated.
- Seasons: Met Office State of the UK Climate (growing season ~3–4 weeks
  longer than 1961–90). UK-generic allowed, disclosed.
- Rain: UKCP18 regional headline changes (summer drier, winter wetter).

Rules: methodology note states which scenario each chart uses (sources do not
share one scenario); any number that cannot be sourced means its section
shrinks or is dropped. No invented projections.
