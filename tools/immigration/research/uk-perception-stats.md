# UK Immigration: Perception vs Reality — Myth-Busting Statistics (compiled Aug 2026)

## 1. Myth: "Immigrants are a quarter of the population"

- **Perception:** Britons' average guess for the immigrant share of the UK population: **24–26%** (Ipsos Perils of Perception series; UK guess 24% in 2014-wave, 25% in later waves). Across 37 countries, average guess 28% vs actual 12%.
- **Reality:** Actual foreign-born share at time of those surveys: **~13%** (Ipsos-cited). Latest census/ONS figure: ~16% (2021 Census England & Wales foreign-born 16.8% — *not re-verified, treat as approximate*). Public overestimates by roughly 1.5–2x.
- **Sources:** [Ipsos "Perceptions are not reality"](https://www.ipsos.com/en-uk/perceptions-are-not-reality) (2014); [Ipsos Perils of Perception 2024 PDF](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2024-11/ipsos-the-perils-of-perception-2024.pdf) (2024).

## 2. Myth: "Most immigrants are asylum seekers / small-boat arrivals"

- **Perception:** **44%** of the public believe refugees/asylum seekers contribute most to UK immigration numbers; **24%** name small boats specifically (Survation, 2025). **59%** overestimate asylum-seeker numbers; **9%** think >75% of migrants are asylum seekers (British Red Cross, 2024). Ipsos/British Future (Jun–Jul 2025): average public estimate that asylum = **33%** of immigration.
- **Reality:** Small boats = **~2%** of people arriving (Red Cross/2023 figure); asylum overall = **~14–16%** of immigration (YE Jun 2025: ~14%). Work + study visas = **83.5%** of new arrivals YE Mar 2025 (>700,000 visas). Computed: 2025 small boats 41,472 vs gross immigration 813,000 ≈ **5%**; peak-year 2022: 45,774 vs ~1.2m gross ≈ **4%**.
- **Sources:** [Survation](https://www.survation.com/myth-reality-and-perception-the-strengthening-of-british-anti-immigration-attitudes/) (2025); [British Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org.uk/about-us/news-and-media/media-centre/press-releases/state-of-the-nation-the-uk-asylum-system) (2024); [British Future/Ipsos tracker](https://www.britishfuture.org/immigration-falling-but-public-still-thinks-it-is-going-up-new-research/) (2025).

## 3. Small boat crossings by year vs total migration

- **Annual detections (Home Office):**
  - 2018: **299**
  - 2019: **1,843**
  - 2020: **8,466**
  - 2021: **28,526**
  - 2022: **45,774** (peak)
  - 2023: **29,437**
  - 2024: **36,816**
  - 2025: **41,472** (provisional; +13% on 2024; 2nd-highest year)
  - 2026: ~**9,000** by end of May
  - Cumulative 2018–YE Mar 2026: **~197,000**
- **Context:** Small boat arrivals = **41% of asylum applications in 2025** (similar share since 2021); 99% of 2025 arrivals claimed asylum or were dependants. Gross immigration YE Dec 2025: 813,000 — boats a small fraction of total migration but dominate coverage.
- **Sources:** [Migration Observatory small boats briefing](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/resources/briefings/people-crossing-the-english-channel-in-small-boats/) (2026); Commons Library CBP-10590 (fetch 403'd; cross-checked via [UK Fact Check](https://www.ukfactcheck.com/article/136/home-office-over-41000-crossed-channel-to-uk-by-small-boat-in-2025-up-13), 2026).

## 4. Myth: "UK takes more asylum seekers than everyone else"

- **Reality:** UK is **5th in Europe in absolute** asylum applications (behind Germany, France, Spain, Italy) but only **~15th per capita**.
- 2024 EU+ average: **~2,200 applications per 1m inhabitants**. Highest per capita: Cyprus & Greece (>7,000/1m), Austria 6,500, Germany 4,000.
- UK 2025: **82,100 applications** (100,600 individuals; slightly down on 2024, 3rd-highest on record). Computed: ≈ **1,200 apps per 1m** (pop ~68.3m) — well below EU+ average. (Per-capita calc computed, not quoted — flag.)
- **Sources:** [EUAA Asylum Report](https://www.euaa.europa.eu/asylum-report-2024/3141-asylum-applications-capita) (2024); [Commons Library asylum statistics](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/sn01403/) (May 2026).

## 5. Myth: "Migrants come for benefits / are a fiscal drain"

- **Perception:** By **2-to-1**, people think immigrants receive more in benefits than they pay in tax (NIESR attitudes review).
- **Reality — eligibility:** Most migrants **cannot claim** most benefits: No Recourse to Public Funds (NRPF) is mandatory on most work/study/family visas until indefinite leave to remain — bars Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, Child Benefit etc.
- **Reality — claim rates (older data, flag):** DWP 2014: out-of-work benefit claim rate **6% non-UK nationals vs 11% UK nationals**. LFS Q1 2016: foreign-born = 17.6% of working-age population but only **7.4%** of out-of-work benefit claimants. No good recent single-source data (nationality not routinely recorded by DWP/HMRC).
- **Fiscal contribution:**
  - OBR (2023): raising net migration 129k→245k/yr lowers primary deficit by **1.1% of GDP by 2072/73**, debt ~30ppt lower.
  - OBR (2024): migrant arriving at 25 on average wages has **more positive lifetime fiscal contribution than a UK-born worker on the same salary**. Low-wage migrants negative, high-wage positive.
  - Oxford Economics (2018, FY2016/17): all migrants **+£4.7bn** net that year.
  - Dustmann & Frattini (2014, 2001–11): recent EEA migrants **+£20.2bn**.
  - Contested by methodology (Migration Watch found negatives); all impacts **within ±1% of GDP**.
- **Sources:** [Migration Observatory fiscal briefing](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/resources/briefings/the-fiscal-impact-of-immigration-in-the-uk/); [Commons Library CBP-7445](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-7445/); [Commons Library NRPF CBP-9790](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-9790/) (2025).

## 6. Myth: "Immigration drives up crime"

- **Perception:** **39%** of Britons believed immigrants increase crime rates in 2025, **up from 30% in 2023** (NatCen/BSA) — belief rising while evidence unchanged.
- **Reality:** Studies find **no average causal impact of immigration on crime** in England & Wales (Bell, Fasani & Machin, LSE). Post-2004 Eastern European migration: no crime impact. Small property-crime effect only for asylum seekers barred from working — a policy effect, not a migrant effect. 23-country European study over 15 years: **no significant link**. Property crime in E&W fell continuously since 2002 while the foreign-born population rose.
- **Sources:** [Migration Observatory crime briefing](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/resources/briefings/immigration-and-crime-evidence-for-the-uk-and-other-countries/); [NatCen](https://natcen.ac.uk/britain-and-immigration) (2025); [LSE paper](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/machin/drafts/crime%20and%20immigration.pdf); [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/does-immigration-really-drive-up-crime-not-according-to-the-evidence-242446) (2024).

## 7. Net migration trajectory (ONS, latest = YE Dec 2025 release, May 2026)

- **Peak (revised):** **944,000** — YE March 2023 (calendar 2022 revised to 872,000, 44% above first estimate of 606,000).
- **YE Dec 2023:** 848,000 (revision churn — flag).
- **YE Dec 2024:** **345,000** first estimate, updated to **331,000** (−59% y/y).
- **YE Dec 2025:** **171,000** (immigration 813,000; emigration 642,000). Lowest since post-Brexit system began early 2021. Driver: non-EU+ work-related arrivals **−47%** in 2025.
- Net migration fell **~82% from peak** (944k → 171k) in under 3 years.
- **Sources:** [ONS bulletin YE Dec 2025](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/internationalmigration/bulletins/longterminternationalmigrationprovisional/yearendingdecember2025) (May 2026); [ONS blog](https://blog.ons.gov.uk/2026/05/21/net-migration-continues-to-fall/); [Migration Observatory on revisions](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/press/uk-migration-data-revised-upwards-yet-again-but-show-downward-trend-in-net-migration-following-visa-restrictions/).

## 8. Salience divergence: concern peaks as numbers fall

- **Ipsos Issues Index:** immigration **#1 issue every month since May 2025**. Sep 2025: **51%** named it a top issue — highest since Oct 2015 (52%). Oct 2025: 47%. Still #1 Feb 2026 (economy 32%, NHS 26%) and June 2026.
- **Divergence:** concern hit its decade-high in Sep 2025 exactly while net migration was collapsing (944k → 171k). Salience tracks small-boat visibility/media coverage (Google searches spike on "small boats" events, flat on "asylum seekers" — UKICE), not total arrivals.
- **Sources:** [Ipsos decade-high concern](https://www.ipsos.com/en-uk/public-concern-about-immigration-reaches-highest-level-decade) (2025); [Ipsos 2026](https://www.ipsos.com/en-uk/immigration-continues-be-seen-most-important-issue-facing-britain); [May 2026 Issues Index PDF](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2026-05/Ipsos%20Issues%20Index_May%2026_v1d2_IUO_EDI.pdf); [UKICE](https://ukandeu.ac.uk/how-small-boat-crossings-shift-public-opinion/).

## 9. Myth: "Immigration is still rising" (it's falling)

- **Perception:** **56%** of Britons thought immigration had *increased* in the past year (Ipsos for British Future, Jun–Jul 2025, n=3,003) — when net migration had actually **more than halved**. Only **16%** expected net migration lower a year on; 38% expected an increase.
- **Reality:** falling steeply throughout 2024–2025 (see §7).
- **Polarisation:** 42% rate immigration's impact negatively (0–4/10) vs 38% positively; "most negative" share (0/10) rose 7% (2021) → 16% (2025) — polarisation rising as numbers fall.
- **Source:** [British Future / Ipsos Immigration Attitudes Tracker](https://www.britishfuture.org/immigration-falling-but-public-still-thinks-it-is-going-up-new-research/) (2025); [full report PDF](https://www.britishfuture.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Noise-and-nuance.Immigration-Attitudes-Tracker-report-2025.pdf) (Nov 2025).

## Verification flags

- 2019/2020 small boat figures: minor source discrepancy (1,835 vs 1,843; 8,461 vs 8,466) — Home Office official tables give 1,843 / 8,466.
- Per-capita asylum rank & rate: "15th place" from press summaries; ~1,200/1m computed, not quoted.
- Small boats as % of gross immigration: 2% is Red Cross (2023 basis); 4–5% for 2022/2025 computed from Home Office + ONS.
- Benefits claim-rate data: most recent robust comparisons are 2014–2016; rests partly on dated data plus current NRPF eligibility rules.
- Fiscal impact: genuinely contested; all estimates <1% GDP either way. OBR most authoritative (2023/2024).
- Foreign-born share "actual": Ipsos used ~13% (survey years); current ~16% not re-verified.
- Net migration figures: subject to repeated large ONS revisions (2022 revised +44%) — always cite vintage.
